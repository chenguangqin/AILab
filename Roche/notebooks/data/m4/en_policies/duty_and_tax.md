# Duty and Tax Policy

## 1. General Principles

All cross-border shipments are subject to the import duties, value-added taxes (VAT), goods and services taxes (GST), and customs processing fees imposed by the destination country's customs authority. Unless an order is explicitly marked as **Delivered Duty Paid (DDP)** at checkout, the recipient shall be the importer of record and shall be responsible for all such charges.

## 2. Major Destination Reference Rates

The following indicative rates are provided for customer reference only. Actual assessment is at the sole discretion of the destination customs authority:

(a) **United States**: De minimis threshold of USD 800 per shipment, per recipient, per day. Shipments below this value generally clear duty-free; however, Section 301 tariffs may apply to certain electronics of Chinese origin, currently ranging from 7.5% to 25%.
(b) **European Union**: VAT applies from EUR 0 (no de minimis) following the IOSS reform of July 2021. Standard VAT rates range from 17% (Luxembourg) to 27% (Hungary). Import duty additionally applies to shipments above EUR 150.
(c) **United Kingdom**: VAT 20% applies from GBP 0; duty applies above GBP 135.
(d) **Japan**: Consumption tax 10% applies to imports above JPY 10,000 declared value; duty rates vary by HS code, generally 0–5% for consumer electronics.
(e) **Australia**: GST 10% applies from AUD 0 under the Low Value Imported Goods (LVIG) regime; additional duty applies above AUD 1,000.
(f) **Canada**: GST/HST 5–15% plus provincial sales tax where applicable; duty applies above CAD 20.

## 3. High-Value Electronics Examples

For reference, the indicative landed cost on a USD 1,200 iPhone 15 Pro Max shipment to the EU may include approximately USD 240–300 in VAT and processing fees. A USD 2,400 MacBook Pro M3 shipment to the UK may incur GBP 480 in VAT plus a small handling fee from the courier. Customers are advised to use the duty calculator at checkout for precise estimates.

## 4. Refusal of Delivery and Refund Implications

If the recipient refuses to pay assessed duties and taxes, the package shall be either returned to the seller's origin warehouse or abandoned at the destination, depending on carrier and customs rules. In either case:

(a) Original shipping fees shall not be refunded;
(b) Return-to-origin freight, where charged by the carrier, shall be deducted from the refund;
(c) Any duties or taxes already prepaid (DDP orders) shall not be refundable, as those amounts have been remitted to the destination tax authority.

## 5. Documentation and Compliance

All shipments are accompanied by a commercial invoice declaring the true transactional value. Under-declaration of value is strictly prohibited and constitutes a violation of customs law in all destination jurisdictions. The seller shall not, under any circumstances, falsify declared values, mark commercial shipments as "gifts," or omit required HS codes.

## 6. Tax-Exempt Entities

Diplomatic, governmental, and certain non-profit entities may apply for tax exemption by submitting valid exemption certificates prior to shipment. Requests received after dispatch cannot be retroactively applied.
